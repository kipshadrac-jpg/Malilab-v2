# Content System and Editorial Production

## Content types and relationships

The product's editorial content includes Playbooks, Modules, Lessons, Practice activities, and references to Lab Tools. Dictionary entries and cross-links may support the core loop where needed, while the full Dictionary remains future scope. Playbooks connect relatable situations to relevant Modules, experiments, and possible next steps. Modules organize Lessons and may reference activities, tools, concepts, Dictionary terms, and Playbooks. Lessons may link terms, activities, tools, and Notes.

This is a product-level content model only. It does not define schemas, content-management software, APIs, or storage.

## Editorial format

Favor short 2–4 line paragraphs, frequent subheadings, bold key insights, lists over walls of text, intentional whitespace, clear progression, examples, practical application, accessible explanations, and Kenyan context. Lessons should be skimmable and visually organized.

Avoid unnecessary jargon, giant text walls, generic US assumptions, unsupported claims, false certainty, guaranteed financial outcomes, and excessive marketing language. The intended curated book/story/library feel refers only to an organized editorial reading and interactive learning experience.

## Research, sources, and review

When content includes regulatory, tax, rates, product availability, or institutional details, the content system should support source attribution and content review. The exact source/review architecture is deferred to a later phase. AI-generated content remains subject to human review before publication unless a future explicit policy changes this.

## Internal AI assistance

AI is an internal research and content-production assistant. It may help the owner structure research, organize and draft Lessons, transform supplied research into structured content, prepare Playbook drafts from supplied scenarios, follow editorial instructions, and speed content production. AI is not the central user-facing product identity; a general user-facing chatbot is not required. An advanced AI user assistant is future scope. Voice and audio functionality are out of scope: no speaker button, voice conversation, text-to-speech, podcast/audio lesson requirement, voice learning mode, or audio-first lesson format.
