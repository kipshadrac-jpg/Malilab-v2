# Modules

## Purpose

A Module is a structured domain of financial knowledge. It contains ordered Lessons and may connect practical activities, Lab Tools, concepts, Dictionary references, and Playbooks. Modules and Lessons are part of initial core scope. The full Dictionary and a large-scale financial knowledge graph are future scope; specific links needed to support the core learning loop remain possible.

## Illustrative domains

The master specification gives Treasury Bills and Bonds, SACCOs, Money Market Funds, insurance, emergency funds, retirement, credit, tax, banking, and business finance as examples. These are not an approved final catalogue or a commitment to cover every topic.

## Possible Module components

A Module may have a cover, title, description, introduction, ordered Lessons, practical activities, Lab Tool references, related concepts, Dictionary references, related Playbooks, completion criteria, and (where applicable) achievement metadata. Exact composition is editorially flexible; this does not require full Dictionary or elaborate badge capabilities in the initial product.

Lessons can progress from an introduction and terminology, through application and practical concepts, toward applying knowledge to a personal scenario. The example progression does not define required curriculum or sequence for any Module.

## Relationships and progression

Playbooks can connect users to relevant Modules; Modules contain Lessons; Lessons can reference Practice, tools, concepts, and Dictionary entries. Related Playbooks can support further exploration. The product allows exploration and does not establish a single mandatory course path.
