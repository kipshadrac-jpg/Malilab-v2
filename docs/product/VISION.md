# Vision and Product Mission

## Product meaning

“Mali” means wealth in Swahili; “Lab” suggests a place for intensive research, experimentation, investigation, and discovery. MaliLab is a Kenyan financial research-and-learning environment.

## Vision

Enable people to research their way toward better financial decisions in the Kenyan ecosystem.

## Mission

Help people study how money works in Kenya, experiment with their own financial circumstances, understand what the Kenyan financial ecosystem can support, and turn learning into a practical financial-freedom direction.

MaliLab is not simply a finance blog, a generic online course platform, or a copy of US-centric personal-finance education.

## Intended user outcome and horizon

After meaningful engagement with MaliLab, the user should be able to define a financial-freedom target, timeframe, assumptions, and possible path based on their own financial circumstances. Approximately three months is an intended product learning horizon for a sufficiently engaged user to reach the point of constructing and investigating this direction. It is not a mandatory completion period, deadline, or promise that the user will achieve financial freedom in three months.

MaliLab must not impose one universal definition of financial freedom. The user defines what it means in their circumstances. Examples include replacing employment income, reaching a desired monthly income, becoming debt-free, building sufficient assets, leaving a particular occupation, reaching a lifestyle target, or creating income independence. These examples are illustrative only.

MaliLab should help the user quantify and investigate their chosen target using their circumstances and assumptions. Calculations are exploratory and educational, not guaranteed financial advice or outcomes.

## Product principles

- Anchor explanations and scenarios in Kenya's financial ecosystem.
- Begin discovery with relevant situations, ambitions, questions, and problems.
- Connect research and learning to a user's own exploratory planning.
- Distinguish education and hypothetical calculations from personal financial decisions.
- Expose assumptions, limitations, and uncertainty; do not promise outcomes.
- Keep unresolved requirements visible rather than filling gaps with guesses.
