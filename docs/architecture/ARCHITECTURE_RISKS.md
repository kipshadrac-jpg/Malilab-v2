# Architecture Risks

**Status:** Initial risk register for review. Likelihood/impact are qualitative architecture assessments, not measured probabilities.

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Trigger to revisit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Content model becomes too rigid for editorially flexible Playbooks/Lessons | Medium | High | Stable content identity plus immutable revisions; flexible typed blocks and optional sections; validate representative editorial content before physical schema. | Editors cannot express approved content without schema changes or opaque HTML. |
| Content block evolution breaks old revisions or notes | Medium | High | Version block schema; keep old published snapshots; stable block IDs; version-aware renderer/fallback; do not rewrite progress references. | Old content cannot render or annotations lose context after a block change. |
| Calculator formula is incorrect, ambiguous, or changes meaning | Medium | High | Formula lifecycle Draft → Tested → Reviewed → Approved → Active → Retired; preserve formula definition, assumptions, expected test evidence, source, reviewer/approval, version, and immutable result snapshots. | A formula can activate without evidence, results cannot be reproduced, source becomes stale, or a formula correction is needed. |
| User Plan revisions overwrite prior assumptions/thinking | Medium | High | Immutable Plan Revision snapshots with predecessor and references to calculation/formula and learning journey context; compare revisions without recalculating old results. | A save mutates history, or users cannot explain why prior and current results differ. |
| Progress becomes a misleading percentage or unexplained badge | Medium | High | Versioned Module requirement set; store explainable facts against exact Module revision and required items; Progress consumes learning/planning evidence one way. | Product cannot state which required Lesson/Practice items and plan attempt support completion/achievement. |
| Historical Playbook context is lost after content updates | Medium | High | Learning Journey Context references exact Playbook revision and recommendation link, then relevant Module/Lesson revisions; no content copying. | A plan/Practice attempt cannot show which scenario and recommendation initiated the learning path. |
| User-owned financial data leaks across accounts or to staff/search/logs | Low/Medium | Critical | Authenticated-owner scoped application services on every path, staff separation, redacted logs, no private search/cache, and required cross-account authorization tests. Initial DB RLS is not required; reconsider if access paths multiply. | Any cross-user exposure, unsafe logging, direct DB/service access, or support process needs broad plan access. |
| AI-generated content is published without human review or has false sourcing | Medium | High | AI output is marked draft; provenance preserved; same human review/publish workflow; no auto-publish; reviewer verifies sources and financial assumptions. | AI provider workflow bypasses review or generated provenance cannot be inspected. |
| Source/rate/institution data becomes stale or provenance changes | Medium | High | Per-content-revision source provenance snapshot and reviewer/timestamp; define review ownership/cadence and display assumptions/limitations. | Content includes time-sensitive values without an owner/current source or historical reference metadata changes. |
| Application/content/frontend domains become tightly coupled | Medium | Medium/High | Modular monolith boundaries, service interfaces, versioned content renderer; avoid direct cross-domain writes and formulas in UI. | A change in one domain repeatedly forces unrelated rewrites or independent release need appears. |
| PostgreSQL search is insufficient for multilingual or relevant discovery | Medium | Medium | Start with rebuildable published-only projection and collect approved quality evidence; keep Search adapter boundary. | UX-approved search relevance, language stemming, latency, or scale misses target. |
| Single-host deployment is resource-constrained or a single failure point | Medium | High | Validate CPU, memory, disk, DB/media capacity and legacy isolation; establish off-host backup and restore validation before production data is considered safe. | Capacity check fails, host conflicts with legacy workload, restore fails, or approved recovery objective cannot be met. |
| Local media storage is lost or fills disk | Medium | High | Storage abstraction, separate persistent media root, media capacity review, off-host backup and restore validation. | Disk growth, restore failure, or multiple app nodes become necessary. |
| Module completion is not explainable after requirement changes | Medium | High | Pin each completion to a Module revision's required/optional/recommended Lesson and required/optional Practice set; preserve satisfied/missing evidence. | An updated Module changes prior completion meaning or a user cannot see why completion was awarded. |
| External provider/hosting choices create privacy/cost constraints | Medium | Medium/High | No external AI/data provider assumed; assess contracts, data use/location, cost, and user-data flows before activation. | Provider would receive user data or introduces material recurring cost. |
| Scope expands into future capabilities and obscures core loop | Medium | Medium | Explicit initial/future boundary; no speculative services or schema; require product decision before expanding. | Payment, advanced analytics, AI assistant, graph, or notifications appear without approved scope. |
| Unspecified account/privacy/legal rules block launch | Medium | High | Keep specific gaps visible; security/privacy review and owner/legal input before handling real user data at scale. | Launch planning begins without deletion, retention, access, or applicable requirement decisions. |

## Highest-priority follow-up

Phase 5C has approved public reading/account requirements, email authentication, hybrid deletion direction, staff roles/MFA and approval separation, KES-first decimal policy, revision-local note anchors, and one-attempt/one-requirement completion. Before their respective feature or production gates, define remaining age/eligibility, per-class deletion/export and backup expiry, calculator-specific formulas/ranges/precision/units, detailed note rendering, accessibility/performance targets, and host/storage/backup capacity. These do not invalidate the modular architecture, but they constrain feature behavior and deployment.
